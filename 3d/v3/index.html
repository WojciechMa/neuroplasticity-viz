Neuroplasticity course: Activity-dependent synaptic refinement in 3D

The same learning rule as on the 2D page, with timing closer to real development. A retinal wave is a short burst of firing that starts at a random spot and sweeps across the retina in a few seconds; waves recur about once a minute, with silence in between. Two eyes now make waves. The modelled ganglion cell of eye 1 sends one axon branch to target A and one to target B, two groups of neurons in the thalamus (the lateral geniculate nucleus, LGN). Other ganglion cells of eye 1 drive target A and a ganglion cell of eye 2 drives target B, so each target fires with the waves of its own eye.

Press Start retinal waves. Between waves the clock fast-forwards, so you don't have to wait a minute; the timeline below the view shows the last five minutes. The synaptic weights change only during the waves of eye 1, one step per wave. Learning is sped up: in the developing brain this refinement takes days.
